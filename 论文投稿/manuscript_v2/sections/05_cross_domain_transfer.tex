\section{Positive and Negative Transfer Across Domains}
\label{sec:cross-domain-transfer}

The same token-normalized KD formulation produces reproducible benefits and harms in different text domains (Table~\ref{tab:cross_domain_transfer}, Figure~\ref{fig:cross_domain_gain}). PTB gains $+0.144937\pm0.003938$ NLL over matched CE; WikiText-2 gains $+0.155665\pm0.007692$. TinyStories instead has signed gain $-0.107219\pm0.000415$. Every domain has the same direction in all three independent student preparations.

The mean CE and KD test NLLs are 4.082352 and 3.937415 on PTB, 4.276364 and 4.120699 on WikiText-2, and 1.582197 and 1.689416 on TinyStories. The negative TinyStories result is relative to a useful alternative: CE improves over $S_0$ in all three seeds. It is therefore not merely a comparison of two unnecessary continuations. Per-seed endpoints appear in Appendix~\ref{app:per-seed}.

These results establish a transfer boundary for the tested configurations, not a causal effect of corpus identity. Teachers, data amounts, and student preparation differ across domains. All accepted endpoints are finite, although clipping and occasional AMP skips limit optimization-based interpretations (Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}).
