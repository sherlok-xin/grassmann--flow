\section{Ensemble and Branch Ablations}
\label{sec:controls}

\subsection{Fused supervision versus component sources}
\label{sec:source-ablation}

Holding Teacher J fixed, fused supervision yields mean gain $+0.155665$, compared with $+0.134868$ for Transformer-only and $+0.040991$ for Grassmann-only (Table~\ref{tab:teacher_sources}). Fused gains exceed component gains in all three seeds: paired differences are $+0.020797\pm0.001214$ over Transformer-only and $+0.114674\pm0.001001$ over Grassmann-only. Fusion is useful in this comparison, but composition and teacher quality change together.

The Transformer-only gate initially clips every update despite finite parameter gradients. Its authorized continuation preserves \texttt{CLIP\_SATURATION\_WARNING}; the relatively small fused--Transformer gap must be interpreted cautiously. The unchanged protocol and formal clipping trajectory appear in Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}. This comparison alone cannot identify heterogeneity as the source of the fused benefit.

\subsection{Homogeneous ensemble control}
\label{sec:homogeneous-control}

TT produces gain $+0.168125\pm0.008948$, compared with $+0.155665\pm0.007692$ for TG. Define
\begin{equation}
H=\mathrm{Gain}_{\mathrm{TG}}-\mathrm{Gain}_{\mathrm{TT}}
=\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{TT}}-\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{TG}}.
\label{eq:ensemble-contrast}
\end{equation}
The mean is $H=-0.012460\pm0.001348$, with all three differences negative. The tested heterogeneous teacher has no KD advantage over the homogeneous control (Table~\ref{tab:tt_vs_tg}, Figure~\ref{fig:ensemble_control}).

TT also has a 0.010108 full-validation teacher-NLL advantage and 6.38\% fewer parameters. Fusion-weight learning rates and training histories differ. These confounds prevent attributing the result to intrinsic TT superiority, but they do not support a Grassmann-specific or heterogeneity advantage. TG exhibits more branch disagreement than TT without producing more transferable supervision (Appendix~\ref{app:branch-diagnostics}).

\subsection{Limits of local diagnostics}
\label{sec:local-diagnostics}

A retrospective analysis asks whether local CE--KD gradients and validation curvature predict archived full-horizon gains. Across seven teacher--domain families and three student seeds per family, first- and second-order scores both achieve AUROC approximately 0.644 and sign accuracy approximately 0.571. They misclassify known positive WikiText-2 families, and curvature adds no sign separation. The tested local diagnostics are not reliable predictors of these endpoints.

Some scalar predictors discriminate the pooled outcomes better, but the small, dependent collection does not establish a general selection rule. Token-level branch JSD likewise adds little predictive information after accounting for CE token difficulty. Calibration stability, full predictor results, and observational limits appear in Appendices~\ref{app:branch-diagnostics} and~\ref{app:local-diagnostics}. These failures concern the tested offline diagnostics, not adaptive distillation methods in general.
