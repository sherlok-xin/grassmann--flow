\section{Problem Setting and Paired Distillability Evaluation}
\label{sec:problem-setting}

We evaluate whether teacher supervision benefits an already-trained autoregressive student. For each seed, a prepared student checkpoint $S_0$ initializes two continuations: maximum-likelihood training with CE and training with CE plus KD from a frozen teacher. Both arms load exactly the same student weights and use fresh optimizers, matched continuation data, and the same training budget. Each arm selects its checkpoint independently by validation NLL. Test NLL is reported only for the selected endpoints.

For a temperature $T$, let $p_T^{(T)}$ and $p_S^{(T)}$ denote the teacher and student distributions obtained by applying softmax to logits divided by $T$. The implemented objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
+ \lambda\frac{T^2}{|\mathcal{I}|}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p_{T,i}^{(T)}\,\|\,p_{S,i}^{(T)}\right),
\label{eq:kd-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}$ contains valid next-token prediction positions after the causal shift and excludes masked targets. Both CE and KL are normalized over valid target tokens; the implementation uses \texttt{token\_mean}, not the legacy batch-normalized KL. The main confirmatory continuations use $T=2$ and $\lambda=5$; the matched CE arm has $\lambda=0$.

Our operational measure of distillability is the paired endpoint difference
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Gain}_{\mathrm{KD}}
=\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{test}}
-\mathrm{NLL}_{\mathrm{KD}}^{\mathrm{test}}.
\label{eq:paired-gain}
\end{equation}
Positive gain indicates a benefit over continued CE training, and negative gain indicates harm relative to that alternative. This comparison controls the starting student within each pair. Comparing KD with an unrelated baseline would also reflect differences in initialization, prior training, or continuation budget. Conversely, positive paired gain does not by itself establish improvement over $S_0$, so we examine that distinction explicitly in the modern stress test.

Confirmatory comparisons use three independently prepared student seeds, 42, 123, and 456. We report individual paired gains, their mean, sample SD, and sign consistency. The SD describes variation across three student preparations; it is not a standard error or a significance test. Teachers and corpus splits remain fixed, so these seeds do not constitute independent replications of teacher training or data collection. Checkpoint eligibility is also part of the protocol: the main comparisons select trained continuation checkpoints, whereas the explicitly labeled modern step-0 diagnostic additionally admits $S_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:external-validity}).
